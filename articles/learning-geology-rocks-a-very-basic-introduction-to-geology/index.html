Learning Geology Rocks: A Very Basic Introduction to Geology

By Andrew Gurry · April 27, 2026 · Geology · Science
From the A-A Events archive. Originally published April 27, 2026 and migrated to Learn Andrew Gurry.

Curious About Geology?

Geology is the study of Earth.

That means geology helps us understand rocks, minerals, mountains, volcanoes, earthquakes, fossils, caves, canyons, and even the dirt under our feet.

At first, geology can seem like a big subject. There are a lot of strange words, old rocks, and huge time periods. But we are going to keep it simple.

This blog series will start with the basics. Then, with each new blog, we will go a little deeper.

Think of it like climbing a mountain.

First, we start at the bottom.
Then we take one step at a time.
Before long, we can look back and realize we have learned a lot.

What Is Geology?

Geology is the science of Earth.

A geologist is a person who studies Earth and tries to understand how it works.

Geologists ask questions like:

Why are some rocks red?
Why do mountains form?
Why are there fossils in dry places?
Why do volcanoes erupt?
Why do some rocks sparkle?
Why does Arizona have so many different kinds of rocks?

Those questions are what make geology fun.

Geology is not just about memorizing rock names. It is about learning the story of Earth.

Every rock has a story.

Some rocks were once melted lava.
Some rocks were once sand.
Some rocks were once mud under an ancient ocean.
Some rocks were changed by heat and pressure deep underground.

When you pick up a rock, you might be holding something that is millions or even billions of years old.

That is pretty awesome.

Rocks, Minerals, and Crystals

People often use the words rock, mineral, and crystal like they mean the same thing.

They are connected, but they are not exactly the same.

A rock is made of one or more minerals.

A mineral is a natural solid material found in Earth.

A crystal is a mineral that grows in a special shape.

Here is a simple way to think about it:

Minerals are like ingredients.
Rocks are like recipes.

For example, granite is a rock. It can be made of minerals like quartz, feldspar, and mica.

Some minerals grow into beautiful crystals. That is why people love collecting gemstones, crystals, and shiny rocks.

The Three Main Types of Rocks

Most rocks fit into three main groups.

Do not worry about memorizing everything right now. We will go deeper into each one later.

For now, just remember these three:

1. Igneous Rocks

Igneous rocks come from melted rock.

When melted rock cools and hardens, it becomes igneous rock.

Lava from a volcano can cool and turn into rock. Melted rock underground can also cool slowly and become rock.

Examples of igneous rocks include basalt, granite, obsidian, and pumice.

Simple idea:

Igneous rock = rock made from melted rock.

2. Sedimentary Rocks

Sedimentary rocks are made from layers.

Tiny pieces of rock, sand, mud, shells, or minerals pile up over time. Those layers get pressed together and become rock.

Examples include sandstone, limestone, shale, and conglomerate.

This is also the type of rock where fossils are often found.

Simple idea:

Sedimentary rock = rock made from layers or pieces.

3. Metamorphic Rocks

Metamorphic rocks are rocks that changed.

They started as one type of rock, but heat and pressure changed them into something new.

Examples include marble, slate, schist, and gneiss.

Simple idea:

Metamorphic rock = rock changed by heat and pressure.

The Rock Cycle

Rocks can change over time.

A rock can be melted.
It can be broken into pieces.
It can be buried.
It can be squeezed.
It can be changed into a different kind of rock.

This is called the rock cycle.

The rock cycle is Earth’s way of recycling rocks.

A rock does not have to stay the same forever. Over a very long time, one type of rock can become another type of rock.

That is one of the big ideas in geology.

Why Geology Matters

Geology is not just something in a textbook.

Geology is part of everyday life.

The roads we drive on come from rocks and minerals.
The buildings we live in use materials from Earth.
The copper wires in our homes come from minerals.
The glass in windows comes from sand.
The batteries in phones and cars need minerals.
The soil that grows food comes from broken-down rock.

Geology also helps us understand natural dangers like earthquakes, volcanoes, landslides, floods, and sinkholes.

So yes, geology is about rocks.

But it is also about life.

Why Arizona Is a Great Place to Learn Geology

Arizona is one of the best places to study geology.

Arizona has deserts, mountains, canyons, caves, old volcanoes, copper mines, gemstones, fossils, and ancient rock layers.

The Grand Canyon alone shows a huge part of Earth’s history.

Arizona also has many different rocks and minerals, including quartz, calcite, copper minerals, turquoise, basalt, granite, sandstone, limestone, agate, jasper, and petrified wood.

That is why this blog series will eventually focus on rocks found in Arizona.

Final Thoughts

Geology is the story of Earth.

Every rock has a history. Every mountain has a reason. Every canyon was shaped by time, water, wind, and movement.

You do not need to be a scientist to enjoy geology.

You just need curiosity.

Pick up a rock. Look at it closely. Ask questions.

Where did it come from?
Why does it look that way?
What is it made of?
How old could it be?

That is how geology starts.

And that is why learning geology rocks.
