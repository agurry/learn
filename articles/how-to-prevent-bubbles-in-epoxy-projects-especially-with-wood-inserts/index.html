How to Prevent Bubbles in Epoxy Projects (Especially with Wood Inserts)

By Andrew Gurry · July 8, 2025 · DIY
From the A-A Events archive. Originally published July 8, 2025 and migrated to Learn Andrew Gurry.

Epoxy is a fantastic material for crafting, woodworking, and DIY projects. From river tables to jewelry molds, the smooth, glossy finish of epoxy resin can give your creations a professional touch. But nothing ruins that glass-like shine faster than a swarm of tiny bubbles trapped inside. 😖

Let’s dive into how to prevent those annoying bubbles, especially when you’re adding materials like wood, leaves, or other porous elements.

Why Do Bubbles Form in Epoxy?

Bubbles can form in epoxy resin for several reasons:

  • Air trapped during mixing
  • Off-gassing from porous materials (like wood)
  • Temperature changes causing expansion of air pockets
  • Pouring too quickly or stirring too aggressively

Even if you mix your epoxy carefully, bubbles will naturally rise up as the chemical reaction begins. That’s part of the curing process. But if you don’t handle it right, those bubbles can get stuck and harden in place—right where you don’t want them.

1. Pre-Seal Wood and Porous Materials

If you’re embedding wood, cork, bark, paper, or anything porous, seal it first. These materials hold microscopic air pockets that release gas into the resin once you pour. That’s called “off-gassing,” and it’s one of the biggest culprits behind bubble problems.

How to Seal Wood Before Pouring Epoxy:

  • Step 1: Apply a thin layer of epoxy resin (or a compatible sealer) over the entire surface of the wood.
  • Step 2: Use a brush or your gloved hand to work the resin into every nook and cranny.
  • Step 3: Let it cure or get tacky before pouring your main layer.

This sealing coat traps the air inside and stops it from escaping later. It also helps with adhesion and prevents discoloration from moisture or uneven absorption.

2. Mix Epoxy Slowly and Carefully

Rapid stirring introduces more air. Here’s how to mix better:

  • Use a flat stirring stick, not a whisk or spoon
  • Stir slowly, scraping the sides and bottom
  • Mix for the exact amount of time recommended by the manufacturer
  • Let the mixture sit for a minute to allow air to rise before pouring

3. Warm the Resin Slightly Before Mixing

Cold resin is thicker and traps air more easily. Warming the resin bottles before mixing (by placing them in warm water for a few minutes) reduces viscosity, allowing air to escape more easily.

⚠️ Don’t overheat — you don’t want to start the curing process early. Lukewarm is perfect.

4. Use a Heat Gun to Pop Surface Bubbles

Right after pouring, many bubbles will rise to the surface. A quick pass with a heat gun or torch pops them instantly.

How to Use It:

  • Turn the heat gun to low or medium
  • Hold it 2–3 inches above the surface
  • Sweep it across the area in a steady motion
  • Don’t linger too long, or you risk overheating the epoxy

💡 Tip: Start at one end and work your way slowly across the surface. You’ll see the bubbles pop like magic.

5. Pour in Layers (Not All at Once)

Pouring a thick layer of epoxy all at once can trap bubbles deep inside. Instead:

  • Pour in 1/8″ to 1/4″ layers
  • Let each layer partially cure
  • Pop bubbles at each stage before adding the next layer

This layered approach gives you more control and reduces the chance of internal bubbles forming.

6. Control the Temperature and Environment

Avoid working in a room that’s too cold or humid. Ideal epoxy conditions:

  • Temperature: 70°F to 75°F (21°C to 24°C)
  • Humidity: Below 50%
  • No dust, wind, or sudden temperature changes

A warm, stable room allows the epoxy to flow better and air to escape naturally. Cold environments slow the cure and trap more bubbles.

Bonus Tip: Let Wood Acclimate First

If you’re working with freshly cut or moist wood, give it time to dry before sealing and embedding. Wet wood off-gasses like crazy and can also affect the curing and color of the epoxy.

Summary

Here’s the quick version:

✅ Seal porous materials before pouring
✅ Stir slowly and carefully
✅ Warm your resin before mixing
✅ Pop bubbles with a heat gun
✅ Pour in thin layers
✅ Keep your room warm and dry

Epoxy is as much an art as it is a science. The more care you take in prep, the better your finish will be.

Final Thoughts

A little prep goes a long way when working with epoxy. By taking the right steps to prevent bubbles—especially when incorporating wood or other porous materials—you’ll get a flawless, professional-looking result every time.
