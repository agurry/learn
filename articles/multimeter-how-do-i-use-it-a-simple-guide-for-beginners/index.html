Multimeter? How Do I Use It? A Simple Guide for Beginners

By Andrew Gurry · May 23, 2025 · DIY
Electrical safety: Beginner practice in this article is limited to low-voltage batteries and completely de-energized components. Do not probe household outlets or exposed mains wiring unless you are trained, using properly rated equipment, and following applicable safety procedures. When in doubt, use a qualified electrician.

What a multimeter does

A multimeter is a measuring tool for basic electrical quantities. In beginner projects, its most useful functions are measuring low-voltage DC, checking resistance, and testing continuity on circuits that are completely disconnected from power.

Start with safety

  • Do not probe household outlets, breaker panels, mains wiring, high-voltage equipment, or unknown circuits.
  • For continuity or resistance tests, disconnect all power and remove batteries first.
  • Use a meter and probes rated for the circuit you are testing, and inspect them for damage before use.
  • If you are unsure whether a circuit is safe, stop and ask a qualified electrician or technician.

Learn on a low-voltage battery

For a simple DC-voltage practice test, plug the black lead into COM and the red lead into the voltage jack. Select DC volts, choose a range above the battery voltage if your meter is not auto-ranging, then touch black to the negative terminal and red to the positive terminal. A small battery is a safer place to learn than building wiring.

Continuity on an unpowered item

With all power removed, continuity mode can help determine whether a simple wire, fuse, or switch provides a complete path. A beep or low reading usually indicates continuity; the exact behavior varies by meter, so consult its manual.

Avoid the current input for now

Current measurement requires placing the meter in series and using the correct input jack. A wrong connection can create a short circuit, damage the meter, or cause injury. Beginners should learn voltage, resistance, and de-energized continuity first under appropriate supervision.

This guide is a conceptual introduction, not a substitute for the manual supplied with your meter or professional electrical training.
